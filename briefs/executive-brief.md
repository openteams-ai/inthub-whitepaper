# The Distributed AI Economy — Executive Brief

*Intelligence Hubs, Frames, Cogs, Ops, and the Accountability Plane · Revision 8 · July 2026*

**The problem: rented intelligence, leaking context.** Enterprise AI is trapped in a paradox. Organizations know AI is strategically decisive, yet most depend on vendor-controlled, black-box systems they cannot inspect, reproduce, audit, or own — and the organizational context that fuels AI's ROI (rules, terminology, goals, style, norms) leaks into prompt one-offs and vendor-side logs. The results are measurable: 56% of CEOs report no financial benefit from AI and only 12% achieve both cost and revenue gains (PwC, n=4,454); only one in five organizations has a mature governance model for autonomous AI agents (Deloitte). The root cause is architectural — the market lacks a standard for what an owned AI deployment looks like, how AI capabilities are packaged into executable units, and how organizational context is encoded, inherited, and exchanged.

**The vision: owned operational intelligence.** Every organization — enterprise, government, research institution, or startup — runs its own Intelligence Hub: a sovereign, governed environment that integrates the organization's data, legacy applications, AI-native workflows, policies, and models within its own infrastructure perimeter. Hubs interoperate through shared open standards rather than a shared owner. OpenTeams is the concierge to the open-source ecosystem: one accountable partner responsible for selection, integration, hardening, maintenance, and evolution — building on the infrastructure each organization already relies on, with no migration required. Nebari is OpenTeams' flagship open-source contribution; Nebi is the reproducibility and distribution mechanism; the platform is standards and open source.

**Execution: Frames, Cogs, and Ops.** Frames are owned, scoped, inheritable artifacts that carry organizational context — each accountable to the humans who manage it. Cogs are governed AI workers oriented by Frames, with defined skills, tools, and permissions — reproducible units distributable via Nebi. Ops are installable, supervised workflows that map onto how people describe their jobs: "Close the books." "Onboard this customer." "Review this vendor for fraud." Models predict tokens; Frames orient the work; Cogs perform it; Ops orchestrate outcomes under human oversight.

**The Accountability Plane: Guards, Gates, and Tracks.** Generation alone does not scale into regulated, high-stakes environments — validation does. Guards are installable, versioned verification components that check whether AI work is correct, safe, and policy-compliant. Gates are decision points where Guard results determine whether work proceeds, pauses, escalates to human review, or stops. Tracks are durable evidence records of what happened — retained under Hub governance, not exchanged. Every Op declares a Validation Strategy; validation runs across the whole lifecycle (pre-flight, in-flight, post-run, continuous). Regulation is converging on exactly this shape: the EU AI Act requires event logging and human oversight for high-risk systems, and Gartner predicts that by 2027, 40% of enterprises will demote or decommission autonomous AI agents due to governance gaps discovered only after production incidents.

**The running example.** An accounts-payable analyst launches one Op: Vendor Fraud Review. Three Frames (company policy, procurement rules, fraud methodology) orient three Cogs (invoice extraction, vendor risk, anomaly summary). Guards check schemas, source grounding, privacy, and cross-Cog consensus; low confidence or high vendor risk is a Gate decision that routes the case to a human reviewer before any vendor is flagged; the complete Track is retained seven years for audit.

**The economy.** The marketplace exchanges four artifact classes — Frames, Cogs, Ops, and Guards — each with its own publishers and dynamics, from freely shared community Frames to commercial Guard libraries encoding regulated-industry expertise. The flywheel compounds: more Hubs create a larger market for publishers; more artifacts make each new Hub more valuable; validated Ops accumulate Tracks, and Tracks compound into demonstrable trust that no competitor can copy.

**Why now.** McKinsey sizes sovereign AI at $500–600 billion by 2030, with regulated industries driving up to 40% of AI workloads toward sovereign environments. The EU AI Act is phasing in through 2028, and 74% of companies plan agentic AI deployment within two years. Organizations that build validation, logging, and auditability into their AI infrastructure now will meet that window; those that wait will retrofit under pressure.
