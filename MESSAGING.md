# Messaging — Approved Short Forms

Canonical language for decks, briefs, web copy, and conversations. Pick the variant that fits the audience; do not improvise new phrasings of the core concepts — extend this file instead. Terminology must match [GLOSSARY.md](GLOSSARY.md).

## The category

**Accountable enterprise AI.** (Not "scalable enterprise AI," not "trusted AI.")

## One-line version

> Frames guide. Cogs work. Ops run. Guards check. Gates decide. Tracks record.

## Slightly more formal

> Frames provide context, Cogs specialize works, Ops perform tasks and orchestrate outcomes, Guards validate results, Gates control action, and Tracks preserve accountability.

## Investor / market version

> The distributed AI economy cannot scale on generation alone. It needs validation. Guards, Gates, and Tracks are the accountability plane that makes AI-generated work usable in regulated, high-stakes, and enterprise environments.

## Product version

> Every Op should declare its Guards, pass through Gates, and leave Tracks.

## Governance version

> Tracks turn AI work into auditable logs that become part of organizational memory.

## The thesis, in brief

> Layer 1 — Infrastructure: the open-source AI ecosystem assembled alongside other products into each organization's Intelligence Hub, with reproducibility ensured by distribution tools like Nebi.
> Layer 2 — Execution: Frames (shared context) + Cogs (specialized AI workers) + Ops (agentic apps).
> Layer 3 — Economy: the marketplace where Frames, Cogs, Ops, Guards, and other management tools are shared and exchanged between Hubs.
> Accountability Plane — Validation: Guards + Gates + Tracks, one plane cutting across all three layers.

> "We don't just provide infrastructure. We define how AI context, work, validation, and outcomes are packaged, executed, verified, and exchanged."

## The opportunity in one sentence

> A distributed AI economy is the Linux + App Store for accountable enterprise AI — where Intelligence Hubs are owned enterprise configurations of intelligence infrastructure; open source (Nebari as a reference) anchors the infrastructure assembled into each Hub, Nebi makes every artifact reproducible and distributable, Frames carry the shared culture and context, Cogs perform the specialized work using models, harnesses, and data, Ops are agentic applications and workflows delivering outcomes from further more specific specialization, Guards and Gates verify and control the work, Tracks preserve the evidence, and products around the Hub make it all usable by the human at work — built by many, OpenTeams among them.

## The canonical relationship (positioning)

> Nebari is OpenTeams' flagship open-source contribution to the AI infrastructure ecosystem. Nebi is the reproducibility and distribution mechanism. The Intelligence Hub is a customer-specific assembly of open-source and proprietary components that OpenTeams integrates, governs, and maintains.

Nebari is our contribution. The AI platform is standards and open source.

## The concierge framing

> OpenTeams is the concierge to the open-source AI ecosystem: one accountable partner responsible for selection, integration, hardening, maintenance, and evolution. OpenTeams does not ask anyone to migrate to a single platform — Hubs are built modularly from the infrastructure the organization already relies on.

## The future-of-software line

> The future is not software alone. It is accountable intelligent operations — built on software, data, models, and governed context.

## The author's stance (the paper's "why listen")

> I have spent nearly three decades building shared abstractions and helping organizations adopt them. None of that was built to be owned by one company; the value accrued broadly, and that was the point. This paper is my argument for making the AI era go the same way.

## The Intelligence Infrastructure framing

> The AI transformation will be defined by the evolution of company information technology (IT) infrastructure into company Intelligence Infrastructure.

> Applied, accountable intelligence has to be intimate with your most important data — and that is only safe when you own and control both the data and the intelligence.

> Intimacy without ownership is exposure.

## The ecosystem framing

> Frames, Cogs, Ops, Guards, Gates, and Tracks are shared abstractions — open standards we promote so that many parties can build to them, open and commercial.

> Every organization is better off owning its Intelligence Hub. The products and services around it — experience apps, compute and model management, Track stores, Gate consoles, Op and Cog builders, Guard libraries, specialized Ops — are an economy built by many and integrated into owned Hubs.

> Horizontal Ops and Cogs serve many industries; vertical specialization is one organization's SOPs made portable through its Frames, Cogs, Ops, Guards, and Tracks.

## The Cogs framing

> Harnesses, skills, tools, and graph layers are the software that makes model weights into a worker. A Cog is where all of that specializes and ships — versioned, installable, auditable. Cog builders choose the model, the harness, and the pre-loaded context; Cog consumers get the AI worker they can build auditable Ops with.

## The agents framing

> The industry built the agent. The Intelligence Hub is where agents are employed — with context, supervision, and a record.

> Most agents are an Op engaging with one ore more Cogs, given identity and memory by the Hub. Whoever holds an agent's context, memory, and history owns the agent. We think that should be you.

> Everyone can build an agent. You need to be the agent employer.
